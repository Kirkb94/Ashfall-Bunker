# Ashfall Bunker

**Run a cramped underground bunker of up to 100 survivors in side-view, defending its doors from ever-stronger wasteland threats while scavenging topside in person, until you can build the signal tower or escape vehicle that gets everyone out.**

**Genre:** 2D side-view tower defense / colony sim with platformer action

**The fantasy:** Being the tense-but-hopeful leader of a living bunker: you manage a crew of individuals with needs, flaws and tangled histories, fight beside them when the doors break, and slowly turn a hole in the ground into a way out.

#### Scope
- Players: Solo for the web prototype; 2 to 4 player co-op in the later PC version, with an optional competitive mode where friends run separate bunkers and race for resources
- Session: About 1 hour per quick run, replayable; the PC version adds a long multi-act campaign across many sessions, with one bunker finished before moving to the next
- Size: Medium. Web prototype first (small slice: one bunker, a handful of rooms, a few turrets, 10 to 20 crew, and only a small subset of the named cast), then a medium PC game built solo over a long time. The full 100-character simulation, co-op, campaign, and the full named cast with cutscenes are staged after the prototype proves the loop. A staged plan: (1) Web prototype: core loop, needs/sleep/schedules, one door, 2 turret types, 3 to 4 rooms, one scavenging level, 3 to 4 named characters. (2) Vertical slice: breach combat, wanderers and revolts, xp, one scripted event, save/load. (3) PC alpha: full bunker size, more rooms and turrets, enemy faction shifts, full first act. (4) PC beta: campaign map, carry-over, remaining named cast and cutscenes. (5) Co-op, then competitive mode, then polish and release.
- Platform: Browser (HTML5/JS) prototype first, then PC (Windows, likely Steam) as the main release

#### Core gameplay loop
1. Check the bunker: crew needs (food, sleep, health), schedules, and what resources are low
2. Assign and schedule crew to jobs (turrets, food stations, med bay, building, sleeping)
3. Build or upgrade outside defenses (walls, turrets) and inside rooms using scrap, parts, electronics and materials
4. Leave the bunker and play a side-view scavenging trip yourself to bring back resources and possibly recruit wanderers, while time keeps running and the crew defends alone
5. Return and survive the next wave or scripted attack, ordering the crew and controlling your own character on the defenses
6. If the door is breached, join a close-quarters side-scrolling fight through the multi-floor bunker, then repair, recover and spend xp
7. Invest in progress toward the escape vehicle or signal tower

Enemies steadily get stronger and shift from a mix of mutated creatures to raiders to robots as the run goes on. Scripted large attacks arrive at set moments. New wanderers appear, each with different stats and a chance to revolt. Crew levels up, resource needs grow with population, and later goal stages demand rarer materials. Relationships between named characters shape events: who works well together, who grieves, who sides with whom during a revolt. In the campaign, each new bunker has a different layout, threats and conditions.

#### Mechanics
- **Crew needs and schedules:** Every survivor has a food gauge (starvation causes damage), health, and a sleep need. The player sets schedules; sleep deprivation applies a debuff that scales exponentially the longer they go without rest.
- **Unique stats and jobs:** Each character has individual stats such as weapon accuracy that determine how well they man turrets or do other jobs. Manning a turret pulls them away from other tasks like food stations or the med bay, forcing tradeoffs.
- **Direct control plus orders:** The player controls one character in a 2D side-view bunker and issues orders to everyone else, such as manning stations or defending a door.
- **Outside defenses and rooms:** Walls and turrets outside the doors are built from scrap and weapon parts. Interior rooms (such as the med bay, which heals and cures illness) need electronics and room-specific parts, plus steel, wire, wood, brick and cement where relevant.
- **Breach combat:** If outside defenses fail at a door, enemies enter and the fight becomes side-scrolling close-quarters combat across multiple floors, with the player fighting alongside the crew.
- **Scavenging trips:** The player personally plays a side-view run through the wasteland to collect resources and find wanderers, while the bunker keeps running in real time and the crew handles threats alone.
- **Wanderers and revolts:** New survivors can join the bunker but each has a chance to revolt, steal resources, and during scripted events even kill crew members.
- **Bonds and rivalries:** Named characters with shared history work better side by side (a small stat bonus when paired on a job or defending together) and react to each other's fates: losing a close friend causes grief debuffs, and a character tied to a rival may hesitate, defect or be swayed in a revolt.
- **Co-op and competitive modes:** 2 to 4 players each control a character in a shared bunker; an optional mode has friends run separate bunkers and compete for resources. Xp scaling is tuned differently for campaign and multiplayer.

#### Progression
Characters earn xp during a run and level up. Between bunkers in the campaign, a few chosen survivors, some gear, unlocks (new rooms and turrets) and character levels carry over. Named story characters gain extra lore, cutscenes and scripted events as the campaign proceeds, and their relationships deepen or break depending on your choices.

#### Winning and losing
Win by completing a goal structure, either an escape vehicle or a signal tower, in the bunker. Lose if all characters die. In the campaign, finishing a bunker moves you to the next one on the map.

#### Setting and time period
A network of underground bunkers dug into a ruined, irradiated wasteland, shown as a cutaway side view with several floors of cramped rooms, ladders, and heavy doors. Outside is a dusty, rusted expanse of collapsed highways and dead towns. Inside, warm lamplight and patched-together machinery contrast with the cold, hostile surface.

**When:** Roughly 15 to 25 years after a nuclear war. It is long enough for mutated wildlife, raider gangs and scavenged or rogue machines to exist, and for a generation to have grown up underground, while old tech is still salvageable.

**Tone:** Tense but hopeful: constant pressure from escalating attacks and fragile crew, balanced by the sense that people working together can reach a way out.

#### Cast
- **Mara Voss**, Player character and bunker leader. Mid-30s, patched utility jacket, welding goggles on her forehead, a worn toolbelt Practical and steady, carries guilt over past losses but keeps people going. Backstory: she led the convoy that founded the bunker and lost her brother to Cinder's raiders on the highway. Old Bram built the bunker's first door with her, Doc Reed treated her after that attack, and Tick was the orphan she pulled from the wreckage and now treats as a younger sibling.
- **Doc Tobias Reed**, Named ally, med bay lead. Older man, thin, round glasses, stained lab coat over armor plates Gentle and dry-humored, stubbornly refuses to give up on any patient. Backstory: a pre-war clinic physician who has known Old Bram for decades (he set Bram's arm after the accident that led to the mechanical brace) and who delivered Nell, the first child born in the bunker.
- **Juno Park**, Named ally, turret specialist and sharpshooter. Young woman with a shaved side of her head, a scoped rifle and ear protection Cocky and sharp, hides nerves behind jokes. Backstory: trained by Sergeant Dorrow at a surface checkpoint before it fell, and she blames herself for leaving her post the night it was overrun. She and Rook have a tense history, since his raiders were in the attack that destroyed the checkpoint.
- **Old Bram**, Named ally, engineer and builder. Large, grey-bearded, mechanical arm brace, covered in grease Gruff and protective, loves the bunker like a person. Backstory: co-founder of the bunker alongside Mara, old friend of Doc Reed, and quietly teaching Tick the trade. He and Dr. Anand argue constantly about how to wire the signal tower, though each trusts the other's work.
- **Cinder**, Rival: raider leader who threatens the bunker and leads scripted attacks. Scarred figure in scrap plate armor with a gas mask painted like a grin Charismatic and ruthless, believes only the strong should leave the wasteland. Backstory: he destroyed the convoy Mara's brother traveled with, took Rook in as a boy and raised him as a raider, and made Vesper his lieutenant after she defected from the checkpoint garrison.
- **Wren**, Recurring wanderer with a secret, may join or betray. Teenager in oversized coat, scavenged satellite-dish backpack Quiet and observant, hard to read, with a tie to the signal tower. Backstory: raised near the dead hilltop beacon and carrying fragments of its encoded broadcasts; Dr. Anand recognizes the signal patterns on Wren's backpack, and Tick sees a kindred spirit in a fellow young runner.
- **The Warden (Unit-9)**, Late-game boss, a rogue defense robot that hunts bunkers. Towering patched-up military machine with a flickering red optic Cold and mechanical, repeating old protocols and broadcasting warnings. Backstory: once the guardian unit of Sergeant Dorrow's checkpoint, still repeating its last orders, and summoned to bunkers by Echo's broadcasts.
- **Sister Odalys Crane**, Named ally, cook and morale keeper who runs the food stations. Broad-shouldered woman in her 50s, flour-dusted apron over a patched flak vest, a ring of keys at her belt Warm and unshakeable, feeds everyone first and lectures them second. Backstory: she works hand in hand with Mags on rations, mothers Nell and Tick, and keeps lamplit evening meals going as a ritual that counters Reverend Finch's gloom.
- **Tick**, Named ally, young scavenger and tunnel runner who knows the surface routes. Wiry kid of about 14 with goggles too big for his face, a rope coil and a bag of rattling salvage Eager and fearless, idolizes Mara and takes risks to prove himself. Backstory: an orphan of the highway attack Mara survived, apprenticed to Old Bram, and the one who first spotted Wren on the surface.
- **Dr. Priya Anand**, Named ally, electronics and radio specialist who works on the signal tower. Slim woman in her 40s, cropped hair, a magnifier visor and a tool roll full of tiny screwdrivers Precise and dryly funny, obsessed with solving the signal puzzle, and short on patience for guesses. Backstory: a pre-war radio engineer who once worked on the beacon network that Echo belongs to, which she hasn't yet admitted to anyone but Wren, whose backpack she recognizes.
- **Sergeant Hale Dorrow**, Named ally, former checkpoint guard who trains the crew for breach fights. Tall, stiff-backed man with a faded armor vest, a salt-and-pepper mustache and a dented riot shield Disciplined and stern, quietly proud of the crew and haunted by the checkpoint he lost. Backstory: commanded the checkpoint where Juno trained and where Vesper once served under him; the Warden was his unit's guardian robot, and he privately carries the guilt of its fall.
- **Fenn Whitlock**, Recurring wanderer and trader who sells rare parts, with a chance to cheat or revolt. Lanky man in a patchwork duster hung with tin cups and trinkets, pulling a rusted cart Smooth-talking and cheerful, always has a deal, and his loyalty is never quite clear. Backstory: he trades with both the bunker and Pike, and sold Cinder maps of Mara's convoy route years ago, a secret that could surface at a bad time.
- **Vesper**, Rival: Cinder's lieutenant who leads raider raids and sabotages the bunker from within. Lean figure in a hooded cloak of stitched tarps, a respirator with cracked green lenses, and a hooked blade Patient and quiet, a strategist who prefers infiltration to open assault and offers defectors a way out. Backstory: a former junior officer under Sergeant Dorrow who left the checkpoint before its fall and joined Cinder, and who quietly recruited Rook's loyalty before he defected.
- **Maw**, Mid-game boss, a huge mutated burrower that tunnels toward the bunker. Armored, blind, pale-skinned beast the size of a bus with a ring of jagged plates around its mouth Wordless and relentless, drawn by vibration and heat, and its arrival is announced by rumbling floors.
- **Lamp**, Narrator and bunker AI whose voice-over delivers status updates and story hints. A cracked amber-lit terminal with a simple pixel face, and a bundle of cables running through the bunker walls Calm, gently hopeful, and a little too formal, with a habit of reading old maintenance logs aloud at tense moments. Backstory: installed by the bunker's original builders, it is maintained by Old Bram and Dr. Anand, and Nell treats it as a friend and reads it her drawings.
- **Reverend Calloway Finch**, Recurring wanderer who preaches that leaving the bunker is a sin and stirs up revolts. Gaunt older man in a scorched coat and a bent preacher's collar, carrying a lantern and a battered book Soft-spoken and persuasive, sincerely believes he is protecting people, and wins followers one worried conversation at a time. Backstory: lost his congregation to the surface, and his sermons clash directly with Nell's dreams of the sky and Sister Crane's warm hopefulness.
- **Pike**, Rival: independent raider who rams bunker doors with a rig and sometimes sells information to the highest bidder. Stocky man in a welded-together armored truck cab helmet, with a spiked battering ram on his rig Loud and greedy, laughs off danger, and can be bribed in a pinch. Backstory: a longtime customer of Fenn Whitlock and a sometime rival of Cinder, whose territory he skirts.
- **Echo**, Final boss and hidden twist: the signal tower's long-dormant counterpart, a distant automated beacon that calls the Warden to bunkers. A towering lattice of antennae and dish arrays on a dead hilltop, pulsing a pale blue light, with a drone swarm orbiting it Silent and indifferent, only ever speaking in repeating encoded broadcasts that Wren and Priya slowly learn to decipher. Backstory: part of the same pre-war beacon network Dr. Anand once worked on.
- **Rook**, Named ally, a rescued former raider who defects and fights alongside the crew. Muscular young man with a half-burned raider banner worn like a scarf, mismatched pauldrons and a cracked bone-handled cleaver Guilt-ridden and watchful, earns trust slowly and is quick to defend anyone who offers him a second chance. Backstory: raised by Cinder, shaped by Vesper, and present at the checkpoint attack that wounded Juno and Sergeant Dorrow; Sergeant Dorrow's willingness to train him is the start of his redemption.
- **Marguerite "Mags" Oyelaran**, Named ally, a quartermaster who tracks supplies, rations and the build queue. Heavyset woman in her 60s with a clipboard, reading glasses on a chain, and a bandolier of labeled pouches Blunt and exacting, nags about waste, and secretly keeps a hidden stash of treats for the children. Backstory: runs the ledgers alongside Sister Crane, distrusts Fenn's deals on sight, and is quietly protective of Nell and Tick.
- **Nell**, Named ally, a child bunker-born survivor who has never seen the sky and wants to. Small girl of about 9 in a hand-me-down jumpsuit with rolled sleeves, a drawing of the sun pinned to her collar Curious, hopeful and stubborn, the emotional heart of the goal. Backstory: delivered by Doc Reed as the first bunker-born child, mothered by Sister Crane, befriended by Lamp and Tick, and the reason many crew members want the signal tower or escape vehicle to succeed.

#### Art direction
Hand-painted-feeling pixel art in a cutaway side view. Warm amber lamplight and soft orange interiors contrast with a cold, desaturated, dusty surface. Characters are readable at small sizes, using strong silhouettes and one signature color or prop each (Mara's goggles, Cinder's grinning mask, Lamp's amber glow). Named characters get more detailed portraits for dialogue and cutscenes; random survivors use modular sprite parts for variety.

#### Assumptions Claude made
- About 60 percent of the named characters are now connected by explicit shared history (the original request); links are written into each backstory above and form three loose clusters: the founding family (Mara, Bram, Reed, Tick, Nell, Crane, Mags), the checkpoint (Dorrow, Juno, Vesper, Rook, Warden), and the signal mystery (Anand, Wren, Echo, Warden).
- Maw is the only named character left without a backstory link, since a wordless beast fits better as an unconnected threat.
- Backstory links drive a light mechanic (paired-job bonuses, grief debuffs, and influence in revolts) rather than a full relationship simulation, to keep the prototype feasible.
- Only 3 to 4 named characters appear in the browser prototype; the rest are added during the PC stages.
- The prototype's 10 to 20 crew and a single bunker are kept from the existing brief as the browser scope.
- The art direction is pixel art with amber lighting, chosen to fit the tense-but-hopeful tone and a solo developer's budget.
- The 1 hour quick run and the campaign are separate modes; the quick run uses a single bunker and a fixed goal, while the campaign chains bunkers.
- Staged plan: web prototype, then vertical slice, PC alpha, PC beta with campaign, then co-op and competitive modes.
